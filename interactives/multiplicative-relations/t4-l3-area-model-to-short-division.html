Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention

We are learning to record division using short division, and to see how each step matches the area model.

Success Criteria

  • I can partition the dividend by place value before I divide.
  • I can record each step of short division and carry what is left to the next place.
  • I can write a zero in the quotient when the divisor does not go.
  • I can check my answer by multiplying the quotient by the divisor and adding the remainder.

Key Idea: Every Digit Is a Chunk

Every digit in the quotient is one chunk of the area model,
written in its place value column.

1

Partition

Split the dividend into hundreds, tens and ones before you divide.

2

Divide

Count in the divisor. Write the digit on top. If it does not go, write 0.

3

Carry

Trade what is left for the next place. Write it small, in blue, in front of the next digit.

4

Check

divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend

🔓 Go Deeper: Zero Hunters

These tasks will push your thinking beyond finding answers. You will explore, make conjectures, and reason like a mathematician.

Specialising
Find three different 4-digit numbers that give a zero in the quotient when you divide them by 6. Remainders are allowed. The zero can be in any place of the quotient, but not at the very front.
Try some numbers with short division. Which ones gave a zero? Which ones did not?
A mathematician's response

I started with 2,466 because it looked friendly. 2 thousands ÷ 6 does not go, so I carried and did 24 hundreds ÷ 6 = 4. Then 6 tens ÷ 6 = 1 and 6 ones ÷ 6 = 1. So 2,466 ÷ 6 = 411. No zero. That one did not work.

Next I tried 1,234. 12 hundreds ÷ 6 = 2, with nothing left. Then 3 tens ÷ 6 does not go, so I wrote 0 and carried the 3 tens. 34 ones ÷ 6 = 5 remainder 4. So 1,234 ÷ 6 = 205 remainder 4. A zero!

I noticed the zero came when the tens digit 3 was smaller than 6 and nothing was carried into it. So I built two more like that on purpose. 4,830 ÷ 6 = 805, because 3 tens ÷ 6 does not go. And 6,036 ÷ 6 = 1,006, which has two zeros.

My three numbers are 1,234, 4,830 and 6,036.

Mathematician's thinking: You are specialising. That means you are trying specific examples to gather evidence, looking for a pattern before you make any claims.
Conjecturing
Kai says: "You only get a zero in the quotient when the dividend has a 0 in it."
Is Kai's statement always true, sometimes true or never true? Test it, then write your own conjecture: when does a zero appear in the quotient?
A mathematician's response

My first thought was that Kai was right. 4,830 has a 0, and 4,830 ÷ 6 = 805 has a zero too.

But then I looked at 1,234 from Task 1. It has no 0 at all, and still 1,234 ÷ 6 = 205 remainder 4. So a zero can appear without a 0 in the dividend.

Then I tried 1,404, which does have a 0. 14 hundreds ÷ 6 = 2, with 2 hundreds left. Carrying them turned the 0 tens into 20 tens. 20 tens ÷ 6 = 3, with 2 left. 24 ones ÷ 6 = 4. So 1,404 ÷ 6 = 234. No zero!

So Kai's statement is only sometimes true. A 0 in the dividend does not decide it. What decides it is the number in each place after you add the carry.

My conjecture: a zero appears in the quotient whenever the number in a place (the carry and the digit together) is smaller than the divisor, so the divisor does not go. The only exception is the very front, where we leave the place empty.

Mathematician's thinking: You are conjecturing. That means you are making a claim that you believe is true, based on your evidence, but you know it still needs to be tested.
Justifying
Mia says 4,832 ÷ 8 = 64. Her working was: 48 ÷ 8 = 6, then 32 ÷ 8 = 4.
Is Mia right? Explain what went wrong using place value, and prove the correct answer.
A mathematician's response

At first I thought Mia might be right, because 48 ÷ 8 = 6 and 32 ÷ 8 = 4 are both true facts.

Then I used my Radar. 4,832 is close to 4,800, and 4,800 ÷ 8 = 600. Mia's answer of 64 is far too small, so something is wrong.

I partitioned: 4,832 = 4 thousands, 8 hundreds, 3 tens and 2 ones. 4 thousands ÷ 8 does not go, so I traded them: 48 hundreds ÷ 8 = 6 hundreds. Next comes 3 tens ÷ 8. It does not go. Mia skipped this place. I write 0 in the tens and carry the 3 tens: 32 ones ÷ 8 = 4.

So 4,832 ÷ 8 = 604. Mia's 6 should mean 6 hundreds, but without the 0 it slid into the tens place and became 60.

Proof: 8 × 604 = 4,832. And 8 × 64 = 512, which is nowhere near 4,832.

Mathematician's thinking: You are justifying. That means you are not just finding the right answer, you are explaining why the wrong answer is wrong and proving the right one.

Plenary: Where Is the Tens Chunk Hiding?

Think, pair, share

Why does the 0 in 609 matter? What would 2,436 ÷ 4 look like without it?

Why the zero matters

Without the 0, 609 becomes 69. The 6 would mean 6 tens, not 6 hundreds. And 4 × 69 = 276, nowhere near 2,436. The 0 holds the tens place.
