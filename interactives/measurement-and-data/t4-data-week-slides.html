Lesson 1 · Reading a Many-to-One Scale

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention

We are learning to read a column graph where one gridline stands for more than one, including columns that stop between gridlines.

Success Criteria

  • I can say what one gridline is worth on a graph.
  • I can read a column that stops exactly on a gridline, and one that stops halfway.
  • I can find a total and a difference from a graph.
  • I can explain why a scale of one would not work for big numbers.
Today

Mini-whiteboards for the Hook, I Do and We Do. Then the Scale Check sheet, then your book under the heading 'Reading a Scale'.

Lesson 1 · Hook

Where does 120 go?

The library lent 120 books on Saturday. Our scale goes up in ones. Where does 120 go?

The problem

The axis would need 120 gridlines. It runs off the board and out the door.

The Pivot

When the numbers are big, one gridline has to stand for more than one. That is a many-to-one scale, and today we learn to read it.

Lesson 1 · I Do

Library loans, Monday to Sunday

1The gap between gridlines is 10. Halfway between gridlines is 5.
1Tuesday reaches exactly the 60 line. Tuesday = 60 loans.
1Tuesday = 60
2Monday stops halfway between 40 and 50. Halfway is 45. Monday = 45.
3Wednesday stops halfway between 80 and 90. Wednesday = 85.
1Total: 45 + 60 + 85 + 70 + 95 + 120 + 110 = 585 loans
2Busiest minus quietest: 120 − 45 = 75
Key idea

Read the scale first. Then read the column against the scale, not by counting lines. A column that stops between lines is read as halfway (or about a quarter, three quarters) between the two values.

Lesson 2 · Choosing a Scale

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention

We are learning to choose a sensible scale for a column graph and draw the graph accurately.

Success Criteria

  • I can look at the biggest value and choose a scale of 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 that fits in about 8 to 12 gridlines.
  • I can label every gridline with equal gaps.
  • I can draw columns with a ruler, including columns that stop between gridlines.
  • I can check my graph against the Graph Checklist.

Lesson 4 · Timelines

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention

We are learning to draw a timeline with equal spaces for equal amounts of time, and to read the time between events.

Success Criteria

  • I can choose a scale for a timeline from the first and last dates and the space I have.
  • I can mark equal intervals and label them.
  • I can place an event between two marks in about the right place.
  • I can work out how many years between two events.
Today

A 20 cm paper strip and a ruler. Your book under the heading 'Timelines', with your scale written at the top.

Lesson 4 · Hook

Where do these belong?

Stick 1903, 1919, 1928 and 1969 where they belong.

11919 − 1903 = 16 years
21969 − 1928 = 41 years

Does the line show that? Not yet. There is no scale.

The Pivot

A timeline is a number line for time. Like a graph, it needs a scale, and equal spaces must mean equal amounts of time.

Card
Lesson 4 · I Do

The history of flight, to scale

On a 20 cm strip.

1Earliest 1903, latest 1969. Start at 1900, end at 1970. Span 70 years.
11 cm = 10 years gives 7 cm. It fits with room for labels.
21 cm = 1 year would need 70 cm. 1 cm = 20 years would squash it into 3.5 cm.
1Marks every 1 cm: 1900, 1910, 1920 ... 1970. Label every one.
11903 is 3 tenths of the way from 1900 to 1910.
11919 is 9 tenths of the way from 1910 to 1920.
21928 is 8 tenths of the way from 1920 to 1930.
31969 is 9 tenths of the way from 1960 to 1970.
1969 − 1903 = 66 years
Key idea

First and last date give the span. The span and the space give the scale. Then equal gaps, every mark labelled, events placed by the scale.

Lesson 4 · Plenary

Timeline or graph axis?

How is a timeline like a column graph's axis? How is it different?

Answer

Both need a scale with equal gaps. A timeline shows when; a graph axis shows how many.

Lesson 5 · Line Graphs and Choosing the Display

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention

We are learning to read and draw line graphs that show change over time, and to choose the right kind of graph for a data set.

Success Criteria

  • I can read a value from a line graph using the scale.
  • I can describe a trend using increase, decrease, steady and steepest.
  • I can draw a line graph with a many-to-one vertical scale and equal time gaps.
  • I can say whether data needs a column graph or a line graph, and why.

A bean plant, measured every week

A bean plant measured every week: 2, 4, 7, 11, 14, 16, 17, 17 cm. What shape will the line be?

The Pivot

A line graph shows change over time. The scale up the side tells us how much; the shape of the line tells us the story.

Lesson 5 · I Do

Drawing the plant graph

1Biggest value 17, so a vertical scale of 2 reaches 20 in 10 gridlines. Weeks are equal gaps across the bottom.
1Week 1 at 2 (on the line). Week 3 at 7 (halfway between 6 and 8). Week 4 at 11 (halfway between 10 and 12).
1Join the dots with a ruler, because the plant was growing between measurements.
1Height in week 5: 14 cm. Growth from week 2 to week 5: 14 − 4 = 10 cm.
1Weeks 1 to 5: increases, steepest between weeks 3 and 4 (up 4 cm in one week).
2Weeks 5 to 7: increases more slowly.
3Weeks 7 to 8: steady. The plant stopped growing.
Language point

It increased from ... to ... between ... and ... The steepest change was ... because the line rises most there.
